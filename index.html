Before you set a level

  1. Move the laptop out from under the bed. A mattress absorbs 500 Hz–4 kHz far more than it absorbs bass, which is exactly the range doing the masking. Under the bed you lose the useful part of the sound and keep the useless part.
  2. Put the speaker between you and the noise, on the floor near that wall, pointed at your head. When the masker arrives from the same direction as the noise, your brain cannot separate the two by direction, and masking works at a much lower level. A masker at 1 m against a source at 12 m is already a 20 dB head start — use it instead of volume.
  3. Earplugs in first, then set the level. The plugs attenuate the masker and the noise equally, so they do not need to be overcome. What they do change is that almost nothing above 3 kHz survives them, so only the 200 Hz–2 kHz layers are doing real work.
  4. Use Listen through to check the room every few minutes while setting up. Stop at the lowest setting that works.

If the laptop stays under the bed: keep the vents clear of bedding, put it on a hard surface rather than carpet, and check it is not getting hot. A machine running 24/7 in a restricted space is a real fire risk, not a theoretical one.

The speaker setting is a 24 dB/oct high-pass. Feeding a laptop driver 60 Hz does not produce 60 Hz — it produces excursion, distortion, and a rattling chassis, so that energy is removed before it reaches the amplifier. Muffling compensation is a broad +dB lift around 1.2 kHz, for when the speaker really cannot be moved out from under the mattress.

A · Deep rumble

Brownian noise, −6 dB/octave, for the bass that survives 12 m and a hard wall. Honest warning: through laptop speakers most of this layer is inaudible, and the high-pass above will remove what the driver cannot produce anyway. It earns its keep only on a real speaker.

B · Voice mask

Speech-shaped noise: pink, high-passed at 180 Hz, emphasised at the focus frequency, rolled off above 3.5 kHz. This is the layer that actually does the work in your setup — it is the only one sitting in the narrow window where noise still gets past both the wall and the earplugs.

Through a wall at that distance, speech arrives with its consonants stripped off and its energy piled up around 300–900 Hz. Start the focus low. Move it up only if you can still pick out individual words rather than just a murmur.

C · High band

Noise held between two movable edges by four cascaded high-pass and four cascaded low-pass stages, about 24 dB/octave on each skirt. Kept for a steady electrical whine. Against neighbours it does nothing, and behind earplugs it barely reaches you.
